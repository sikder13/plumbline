# Wave 3 inspiration board

Studied on 14 October 2026: 20 sites, 14 from Awwwards (Site of the Day, Developer Award, Honorable Mention) and 6 from the Framer Gallery, all featured in 2025 or 2026.
Each site was opened in Playwright at 1440 and 390 wide, scrolled top to bottom (wheel or inner scroller where the site hijacks scroll), and its markup and loaded font files read for faces, motion and booking links.
Awards and dates were confirmed on each Awwwards entry page or Framer Gallery page. Screenshots live under `previews/review/wave-3/inspiration/` (not committed) and are for designers' reference only. Borrow patterns and thinking, never assets, code or whole layouts.

## Tour Kyrgyzstan in depth

Awwwards Honorable Mention, 6 September 2026. One founder, five fixed tours plus a custom option, built in plain HTML, CSS and vanilla JS. Studied on the home page and the Celestial Lakes tour page, at desktop and phone width.

**Storytelling**
- It opens on a person, not a product. A white screen types "Kyrgyzstan is home to us. We'd love to show it to you." letter by letter with a blinking cursor, then fades into the hero.
- The hero sells an angle: "Central Asia like you've never imagined", subline "Journeys which don't exist in guidebooks". The looping, muted hero video cuts between Tian Shan scenes (looking up a gorge at the sky, sheep grazing a high pasture).
- The second beat is the founder in first person: journalist, documentary maker, certified mountain guide, beside a halftone cutout portrait. Trust comes before the catalogue.
- Order after that: five tours, a film, the film production side business, named reviews, eight FAQs, and a footer letter that ends "you don't need a guidebook; you need a trusted friend."
- Copy is short and concrete: days, real place names, honest effort levels ("not a walk in the park").

**Motion**
- Nothing moves unless it explains something. The typed intro runs at about 30 to 50 ms a letter with longer pauses at word ends, then a 0.6 s fade.
- The tour selector is the centrepiece. A section six screens tall pins a three column stage: tour names with day counts on the left, one framed photo in the centre, the description on the right. Scrolling steps through the tours. The photo strip glides so the active image sits inside red corner brackets while the images above and below fade to white. The active name turns red. The description scrambles through symbols for about a third of a second, then resolves left to right. When you stop, it snaps to the nearest tour.
- 24 hairline ticks on both screen edges work as a scroll gauge, like an altimeter. The current tick stretches from 14 to 30 px and turns red.
- Hovering a production service row floats a photo next to the cursor. Moving between pages wipes a near black panel across the screen in half a second.
- Wheel scrolling gets light momentum; it is switched off for touch screens and for reduced motion.

**Typography**
- Two faces. Archivo ExtraBold in capitals for headlines, leading under 1, slight negative tracking: blunt and poster like. Fragment Mono for everything else, in capitals at 12 to 13 px with justified paragraphs: a field notebook or film slate voice.
- Buttons and nav sit in square brackets, "[ DISCOVER TOURS ]", "[ GALLERY ]", which reads like stage directions and echoes the crop marks around photos.
- One accent, a hot red orange (#DC2F02), on white and near black (#0D0D0D).

**How it guides a visitor to book**
- No cart, no date picker. Every path ends in a short message: the red hero button, the pinned tour list, and "Create your own adventure" as a sixth tour shown as a solid red frame with a crosshair.
- The first FAQ is "How do I book? Is it hard?", answered in two sentences: send the tour and dates, we reply within 24 hours.
- A tour page opens with a fact strip (Duration 10 days, Difficulty Moderate, Season June to Sept, Price €1600 with an [i] note) that docks to the top as you scroll, then a day by day list, then one red "Request full itinerary" button that opens a photo plus email form. Email and WhatsApp sit in the footer.
- On phone the pinned selector becomes a swipe row of framed cards with Prev and Next joined by a rule, the founder text becomes a two page card, FAQ becomes a plus sign accordion, the side gauge is hidden, and the hero button drops to the bottom of the screen within thumb reach.

## Site by site

### Tour Kyrgyzstan (TK)
- Hero: full screen video under a 45% black veil; heavy caps headline on the left third, a mono note "TOURS & FILM PRODUCTION" top right, a short mono paragraph bottom right, one red bracketed button bottom left.
- Scroll story and signature interaction: person, tours, film, proof, questions, then a signed letter in the footer; the pinned tour selector with its framed photo strip, scrambling description and scroll snap is the heart (see the in depth section).
- Typography and colour: Archivo ExtraBold caps with Fragment Mono caps, a film slate voice; white and near black sections alternate, red orange #DC2F02 is the only accent, photos carry a warm faded film grade.
- Navigation and booking flow: logo left, two bracketed links right, header text flips colour with the section beneath; booking is message first, then a tour page fact strip, day list and "Request full itinerary" email modal.

Patterns worth borrowing:
1. TK-1 Typed welcome: a blank screen types one or two warm lines in mono with a cursor, then fades away. Under three seconds.
2. TK-2 Viewfinder corners: thin red L shaped brackets at the four corners of every photo and card, so every image reads as footage.
3. TK-3 Pinned tour selector: tall section pins list, framed photo and description side by side; scrolling steps through tours and snaps.
4. TK-4 Edge gauge: two columns of hairline ticks at the screen edges; the current one grows and turns the accent colour as you scroll.
5. TK-5 Scramble resolve: a changing paragraph flickers through symbols for a third of a second, then settles into the new text.
6. TK-6 Bracket labels: buttons and nav written as "[ LABEL ]" in mono caps.
7. TK-7 Custom trip as the last tour: the final item in the tour list is "Create your own", shown as a solid accent tile.
8. TK-8 "How do I book? Is it hard?" as the first question, answered in two plain sentences.
9. TK-9 Docking fact strip: duration, difficulty, season and price in one row that sticks to the top of a tour page.
10. TK-10 Soundtrack tape: a small cassette icon in a corner opens a short playlist of local music, off until tapped.

## What these sites avoid

- No hero slideshow with dots and arrows. Most open on one film or one photograph and one line; Lingers cross fades a few photos with no controls at all.
- No stock people. The guide, the winemaker, the family and their dog appear in their own candid photos.
- No more than one accent colour, no gradients on buttons, no drop shadows on cards.
- No form before trust. The first booking step is a short message, a date pair or a call; the long form, if any, comes later.
- No hidden prices when the price is simple: Tour Kyrgyzstan €1600, Icebike ISK per person, Lingers from €115, White Desert in US dollars.
- No autoplaying sound. OceanX and Tour Kyrgyzstan wait for a click before playing anything.
- No walls of review widgets. Reviews are quoted with a name and a source.
- The weaker moments are worth avoiding too: Lost Horse's newsletter pop up over the first scroll, Bijou's age gate before the first photo, and cookie banners on Lingers and Pop-Up Hotel that cover half a phone screen.
